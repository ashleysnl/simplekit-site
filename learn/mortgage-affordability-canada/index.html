Mortgage Affordability in Canada

Mortgage affordability is not just a house-price question. It is a relationship between income, existing debt, down payment, mortgage terms, taxes, heating costs, and the rate a lender uses to test whether the payment is manageable.

Start with the payment, then widen the view

Income sets the ceiling

Gross household income is the starting point for most affordability screens. A higher income can support a larger payment, but existing debt and housing costs reduce the room available.

Debt service ratios matter

Canadian mortgage underwriting commonly uses Gross Debt Service (GDS) for housing costs and Total Debt Service (TDS) after adding other debt obligations. These are lender qualification tools, not household comfort targets.

The stress test changes the qualifying payment

For many mortgages, qualification uses a rate higher than the contract rate. That means the payment you can afford in day-to-day cash flow may be larger than the payment a lender will qualify.

Ownership costs extend beyond principal and interest

Property tax, heating, condo fees, insurance, maintenance, and repairs do not disappear because the mortgage payment fits. A realistic home budget should leave room for those costs too.
