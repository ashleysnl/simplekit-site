Credit Card Interest and Payoff

Credit cards are flexible, but carrying a balance can make small purchases expensive over time. The key variables are the balance, annual interest rate, required payment, and how much extra you can consistently send to principal.

Why revolving credit can linger

Interest keeps working against the balance

When you carry a balance, interest adds borrowing cost while your payment is trying to reduce principal. A higher rate leaves less of each payment available to shrink the debt.

Minimum payments are designed to keep the account current

A minimum payment is not a payoff strategy. Depending on the balance and card terms, paying only the minimum can stretch repayment over a long period.

Extra payments attack principal sooner

Paying more than the required amount reduces the balance earlier, which reduces the amount on which future interest is charged.

New purchases can erase progress

A payoff forecast assumes the balance follows the model. Continuing to add purchases can extend the timeline even when every scheduled payment is made.

Use the payoff estimate as a decision tool

Compare payment levels

Testing several monthly payments shows how much time and interest a manageable increase could save.

Compare expensive debts together

If you have multiple balances, the interest rate and minimum payment on each debt help determine which one deserves extra cash first.

Be careful with consolidation

A lower-rate loan can reduce interest, but only if fees, term length, and new borrowing do not erase the benefit.
