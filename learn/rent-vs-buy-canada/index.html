Rent vs Buy in Canada

Renting and buying solve the same housing need in very different ways. A useful comparison looks beyond the monthly rent and mortgage payment to the full cash-flow and wealth-building tradeoff over the time you expect to stay.

Compare the full cost of each path

Rent is more than a monthly cheque

Rent can rise over time, but renters usually avoid property tax, major repairs, and many transaction costs. The key question is what happens to the cash that is not tied up in ownership.

Buying is more than the mortgage payment

Interest, property tax, maintenance, insurance, transaction costs, and the opportunity cost of the down payment all matter. Principal repayment builds equity, but interest and many ownership costs do not.

Time horizon can dominate the answer

Buying has meaningful upfront and exit costs. A short stay can make those costs harder to recover, while a longer stay gives home equity and appreciation more time to matter.

Investing the difference keeps the comparison fair

If renting costs less each month or requires less upfront cash, the comparison should model what happens if that difference is invested rather than simply spent.

Treat the result as a sensitivity test

Home appreciation is uncertain

Small changes in assumed appreciation can materially change the long-run result. Test a range rather than relying on one optimistic number.

Investment returns are uncertain too

A higher assumed market return can favour renting if the renter invests the difference, but actual returns vary and arrive unevenly.

Lifestyle still matters

Control over the property, mobility, school plans, renovation preferences, and tolerance for maintenance can matter as much as the spreadsheet result.
