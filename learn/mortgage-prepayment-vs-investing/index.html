Mortgage Prepayment vs Investing

Paying down a mortgage and investing can both strengthen a household balance sheet, but they create different kinds of value. The cleanest comparison separates the guaranteed return from avoided mortgage interest from the uncertain after-tax return of investing.

What extra mortgage payments give you

A guaranteed interest saving

An extra principal payment reduces future interest at the mortgage rate. Unlike an investment return, the interest avoided is known once the payment is made.

Lower debt and fixed obligations

A smaller balance can reduce renewal risk and move the debt-free date forward. That can matter even when investing has a higher expected return.

Less liquidity

Money paid into the mortgage is tied up in home equity. Accessing it later may require refinancing, a line of credit, or selling the property.

What investing gives you

Higher expected return, but with uncertainty

Diversified investments may outperform the mortgage rate over long periods, but returns are not guaranteed and market declines can occur at inconvenient times.

Tax treatment can change the comparison

A TFSA can shelter future investment growth from tax, while RRSP contributions can create a deduction now and taxable withdrawals later. Taxable accounts add another layer of tax drag.

Liquidity and flexibility

Invested assets are generally easier to redirect toward another goal than home equity, although market value can be lower when you need the money.

A useful decision rule

Compare after-tax expected return with the mortgage rate

The mortgage rate is a useful hurdle because prepaying avoids that interest with certainty. Investing needs to beat the hurdle after fees and taxes to create a financial advantage.

Then include risk and preference

If the numbers are close, the better choice may come down to your tolerance for debt, need for liquidity, investment horizon, and comfort with volatility.
