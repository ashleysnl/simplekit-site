Compound Interest and Investment Fees

Compounding can make long time horizons powerful because growth can earn additional growth. Fees work in the opposite direction: money removed from the portfolio no longer has the same opportunity to compound.

What drives compound growth

Time gives compounding room to work

The longer money remains invested, the more periods there are for returns to build on earlier returns. That is why starting earlier can matter even when contributions are modest.

Contributions often matter more than tiny return tweaks

A consistent savings habit adds fresh capital to the compounding base. For many plans, increasing contributions is more controllable than trying to predict a slightly higher return.

Returns are assumptions, not promises

A smooth annual growth rate is useful for modelling but real markets fluctuate. Test lower and higher return scenarios rather than treating one forecast as certain.

Why ongoing fees deserve attention

A fee reduces the return that stays invested

If two otherwise identical investments earn the same gross return, the one with lower ongoing costs leaves more money in the account to compound.

Small annual differences accumulate

A fee that looks small in one year can create a much larger ending-value gap after decades because the lost dollars also lose future growth.

Cost is not the only investment criterion

Risk, diversification, taxes, suitability, trading behaviour, and the investment itself still matter. Fees are one important variable, not a complete investment decision.
