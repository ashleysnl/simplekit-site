Debt Snowball vs Avalanche

The debt snowball and debt avalanche use the same basic idea: keep making minimum payments on every debt and direct extra cash to one balance at a time. The difference is which balance gets attacked first.

Two payoff orders, two different advantages

Debt avalanche: highest rate first

The avalanche directs extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate. If payments and timing are otherwise identical, this generally minimizes interest cost.

Debt snowball: smallest balance first

The snowball targets the smallest balance regardless of rate. Clearing an account sooner can create visible progress and free one minimum payment to roll into the next debt.

Minimum payments continue everywhere else

Both methods depend on keeping every account current while concentrating extra payments. Missing required payments can create fees, credit damage, or higher borrowing costs.

The best plan is one you can sustain

The avalanche can be mathematically cheaper, but a method that causes you to quit is not useful. Compare the dollar difference and decide whether faster psychological wins are worth any extra interest.

Make the payoff plan stronger

Stop adding expensive balances

A payoff schedule cannot make progress if new high-interest borrowing replaces the principal you just repaid.

Use windfalls deliberately

Tax refunds, bonuses, and other irregular cash can shorten the payoff period dramatically when they go directly to principal.

Protect a small cash buffer

Without any emergency cash, a routine surprise can push spending straight back onto credit. Debt payoff and resilience need to work together.
